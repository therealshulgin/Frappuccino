# Threat model, motto & trust boundaries — Design rationale (adversarial steelman)

> Model: claude-opus-4-8[1m]. Date: 2026-06-28. Scope: the foundational design choices — phone-as-relay-not-vault, the scoped meaning of "une saisie n'expose rien", the semi-trusted/hostile relay, the named threat actors, and the deliberate out-of-scope decisions — and whether each is defensible to a future adversarial audit.

This document is a **design-defense**, not a vulnerability hunt. For each foundational choice it makes the affirmative case, justifies the specific implementation against alternatives, states the sharpest honest objection and either rebuts or concedes it, then surfaces the assumptions and the questions a future auditor should probe. Where the prose docs and the live code diverge, **the code is treated as authoritative** and the divergence is flagged — that is the single most load-bearing finding of this domain (see §3 and the SNI tension throughout).

---

## 1. Scope & the choices under review

The "threat model & motto" domain is where Frappuccino decides *what it is defending, against whom, and what it deliberately refuses to defend*. The concrete choices:

- **C1 — Phone is a relay/transmitter, not a vault.** Footage leaves the device during capture; nothing readable persists locally. Stated as the motto and as the product thesis in `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:3-5,21`, `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:61-72,77`.
- **C2 — "Une saisie n'expose rien" is scoped to *identity + content*, NOT volume/cadence/timing.** Explicitly bounded in `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:111-118` and `docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:114`; the metadata residual is mapped in `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:33,36,§4`.
- **C3 — The relay is semi-trusted / hostile-at-rest by design.** Server seizure is a *design input*, not a failure mode; the operator (including the project) is treated as potentially hostile. `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:98-118`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:28`, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:84-94` ("boîte noire hostile"), enforced in code by the Phase-C identity-free relay (`server/app/routes/reports.py:18,23`).
- **C4 — Explicit graded threat actors.** On-path DPI/passive observer, active MITM with valid CA, device seizure with PIN known vs unknown, device seizure mid-recording, relay seizure / hostile operator, coercion of PIN, coercion of phrase. Tabulated in `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:104-119`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:41-48`, `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:18`, `docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:21`.
- **C5 — The sovereign key is a 12-word paper phrase; the private read key exists on no machine.** A deliberate single-point-of-total-failure concentrated on one offline artifact. `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:120-139`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:29,55`, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:99-102`.
- **C6 — Deliberate out-of-scope: traffic envelope / "fact of emitting", compromised OS at capture, court-grade chain of custody, burned domain, an active state holding the APK.** `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:291-306`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:50-56`, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:116-117`, `docs/TLS_PINNING_ROTATION_RUNBOOK.md:26`.
- **C7 — The trust boundary the motto rests on: a pinned-TLS channel to a single relay endpoint.** SPKI pin + host check in `crypto-rs/stream/src/pin.rs:36,69,209-248`, mirrored in `mobile/src/main/res/xml/network_security_config.xml:28-61`.

## 2. Choice-by-choice rationale

### C1 — Phone is a relay/transmitter, not a vault

- **What it is.** The product thesis: "Le téléphone est un émetteur. Pas un coffre." (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:77`; `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:3,21`). Recording is cut into 5-second chunks, each E2E-encrypted on-device and uploaded immediately; on a new device the witness re-derives identity from the paper phrase and pulls the blobs back (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:84-89,126-129`).
- **Affirmative case.** This is the *only* one of the three field exigencies that the classic answer cannot meet. A local encrypted vault fails the snatch-mid-recording case (a file sealed "at the end" protects nothing if capture is interrupted) and fails the seizure-then-wipe case (the content dies with the device). The relay model makes the phone — the exact artifact the adversary physically holds — *not* the single point of failure (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:46-72`). It directly serves the motto: everything leaves fast, a seizure of the phone yields nothing to read.
- **Why this implementation.** The alternative explicitly named and rejected is the Tella-style "coffre au repos" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:242-248`): better-encrypting a vault still leaves the data and its key co-located on the seized device. A "send a posteriori" model (Tella, eyeWitness) is rejected because it loses the mid-capture window (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:217`). Streaming 5-s chunks during capture is the mechanism that turns "the past 12 minutes" into "already out of reach" rather than "still on the phone."
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* "relay not vault" is rhetoric — the phone still holds plaintext frames in the encode pipeline, a staging MP4 on crash/force-kill, and the live ratchet/derived key in RAM while unlocked or recording, so a seizure of a *live* device exposes plenty. *Disposition — partial concession.* The motto's claim is scoped to **past content** and to a *seized-then-examined* (AFU/wiped) device, not a live unlocked one. The auditor dossier concedes the live-RAM and staging-window surfaces openly (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:599-603` item 4, R-D-2 at `:668-672`). The defense is honest precisely because it does not claim to cover the live-device case — it claims past rushes are gone, and forward secrecy (proven separately) backs that.
- **Assumptions & residual.** Rests on: chunks actually being deleted after upload (the doc says secure-deleted after `204`, `docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:165`); the relay actually retaining them (availability is *not* crypto — it is redundancy, and off-host backup is roadmap 1.8, undone); and the upload path keeping pace with capture on bad networks. Does NOT claim to protect a live unlocked device or to guarantee delivery from a single hostile relay.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) Is there any plaintext-MP4 staging window on crash/force-kill that survives, and what touches `filesDir`/`cacheDir` in clear (protected only by FBE, not by app crypto — `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:710-712`)? (2) After a `204`, is the local chunk *securely* deleted, and what is the wear-leveling residual on flash?

### C2 — "Une saisie n'expose rien" scoped to identity + content, not volume/cadence

- **What it is.** The motto's strong claim is deliberately bounded: a seizure exposes *no identity and no testimony content*, but an operator still sees **volumes and timestamps** of opaque blobs — the *number* of objects and their *cadence*, never the *who* or the *what* (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:111-118`). The relay-blind redesign explicitly re-states this: "une saisie relais n'expose toujours aucune identité ni contenu… ce qui subsiste… c'est le nombre d'entrées et leur cadence" (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:114`).
- **Affirmative case.** Scoping the claim is what makes it *true* and therefore credible to a population in danger. An unscoped "a seizure exposes nothing" would be a lie the first auditor catches; the bounded version survives inspection. The exposure map operationalizes the boundary: content is sealed and (post Phase C) the at-rest identity↔report↔"when" join is *removed* (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:4,68-74`), leaving an "*some* capability filmed at T1…T3" timeline keyed by a phrase-derived `report_id`, not by a pk (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:74`).
- **Why this implementation.** The honest alternative — claim full metadata protection — would require padding/cover-traffic and a crowd (shared CDN front or multi-tenant VPN), both rejected near-term: padding is "coûteux en données mobiles + batterie" and the destination-unlinkability problem has "pas de repas gratuit" (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:131,145`). So the project ships the achievable guarantee (content + at-rest identity) and *names* the residual rather than over-claiming. Phase C went further than the motto strictly requires by stripping `owner`/`author`/`createdAt` from `reports.json` (`server/app/routes/reports.py:18,23`) — narrowing the residual to enrolment-registry counters and MinIO mtime.
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* volume + cadence + a fixed destination IP is often *enough* to corroborate a raid or place a person at an event — "this device filmed 47 chunks on 2026-05-20 14:23 UTC" is itself dangerous (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:18`). So "n'expose rien" is doing a lot of work for a guarantee that leaves a usable signal. *Disposition — conceded and documented.* The map rates the on-wire timeline MEDIUM/EXPOSED-BY-DESIGN (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:28,66`) and the MinIO size/mtime fingerprint HIGH/EXPOSED-BY-DESIGN (`:33`). The defense is that this is a *stated non-claim*, not a hidden gap — and the motto's word "rien" is explicitly redefined to mean "no testimony, no person," not "no signal" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:117-118`).
- **Assumptions & residual.** Rests on the relay never persisting the client IP (verified CONTROLLED in `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:§3`), on the enrolment registry holding only counters not a report map (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:46,148`), and on the directory's opaque entry names (M-1) hiding the session counter from an at-rest snapshot (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:109,114`). Does NOT claim traffic-analysis resistance or unlinkability of *which IP* uploaded *when*.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) Does the rescue **directory** report (one `report_id` aggregating all of a witness's session entries) re-introduce an at-rest "this capability filmed N sessions at this cadence" object that, once correlated, partially undoes the Phase-C identity-stripping (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:114` non-claim M-1)? (2) Is the per-pk enrolment counter (`ratchet_registry.json`) genuinely just a number, with no derivable link from a pk to a `report_id`?

### C3 — The relay is semi-trusted / hostile-at-rest by design

- **What it is.** The relay stores only opaque blobs; the X25519 private read half "n'existe sur aucune machine" — not the phone, not the server (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:98-104`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:28`). "La saisie du serveur est une donnée d'entrée du design, pas un cas d'échec," and the operator "nous compris" is treated as potentially hostile (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:107-109`). The auditor guide instructs treating the Python relay "comme une boîte noire hostile — sa compromission ne doit pas livrer de clair" (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:84-90`). Enforced in code: Phase C made `reports.json` identity-free (`server/app/routes/reports.py:18,23`) and archive reads identity-free (no `require_archive_auth` on the routes, `server/app/routes/archive.py:59,68`).
- **Affirmative case.** This is the structural answer to "a server can be subpoenaed, hacked, or run by a traitor": a relay cannot betray what it cannot read. It removes the project itself from the trust base — the strongest possible posture for a tool whose users cannot afford to trust its operators. It directly enables the "seizure exposes nothing" claim end-to-end: even with full disk + RAM image, only blob *content* stays sealed (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:85`).
- **Why this implementation.** The rejected alternative is the institutional model (eyeWitness): a server that *must* read to certify, where trust and custody rest on the institution (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:279-288`). Frappuccino picks the inverse trade — "personne d'autre que le témoin ne peut lire, personne n'a à être cru" — at the explicit cost of court-grade provenance. `crypto_box_seal` to an x25519 key whose private half is phrase-derived is the mechanism that makes "blind" structural rather than policy-based.
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* "blind at rest" is undermined by the *active* operator who modifies the code to start logging the transient pk seen at report creation (the 1st PUT carries a `sub=pk` JWT, `docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:48,147`), or who is in a US CLOUD-Act jurisdiction (Vultr) and can be compelled to image RAM where the client IP is live for rate-limiting (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:18,52-53`). *Disposition — conceded as a live/active-operator axis.* The "blind" claim is explicitly an **at-rest** claim; the transient-creation pk and the live-RAM IP are named residuals (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:147`, `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:§3 caveats`). The honest framing: a hostile operator who *modifies code going forward* can capture *future* transients, but a seizure of the *current* disk yields no identity↔content link.
- **Assumptions & residual.** Critically depends on `--workers 1` (the nonce/anti-replay cache is in-process; multi-worker would reopen concurrency safety, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:207-208,701-702`); on the JWT HS256 secret staying confidential and never logged (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:705-706`); and on the operator not retroactively adding logging *before* the seizure window of interest. Does NOT claim protection against an operator who instruments the live process to capture transients.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) Trace every path by which the transient creation-time pk (`sub` in the stream JWT) could reach disk via an exception, `repr`, or positional log arg — `docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:44,187` flags this was *missed by two prior audit passes*; is the global exception handler + lint actually in place and effective? (2) Confirm no route leaks `request.client`/IP into any persistent log under any error path (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:53,109`).

### C4 — Explicit graded threat actors

- **What it is.** A capability-by-capability table of adversaries with an explicit Protected? verdict and mechanism per row (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:104-119`), echoed user-facing in `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:41-48` and metadata-side in `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:18` (five graded adversaries a→e). Includes the two PIN-coercion rows that are *deliberately not protected*: "Saisie appareil, PIN connu → Non (chemin assumé)" (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:108`).
- **Affirmative case.** Writing the threat model "noir sur blanc," including the rows marked **Non / Hors**, is what lets an auditor *re-qualify* any row rather than discover an unstated assumption (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:624-628`). The graded ladder (passive → coercion → Dolev-Yao → hostile relay → supply-chain, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:120-122`) maps each adversary to the specific mechanism and, for the crypto core, to a machine-checked proof — turning the threat model into a verification index, not a marketing list.
- **Why this implementation.** The alternative — a prose narrative of "we protect against bad guys" — is unfalsifiable and useless to the next auditor. The table form with `AUDIT_SCOPE_RUST.md §3` as the authoritative source (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:120`) makes each claim individually testable and ties it to a runner. The PIN-known row is honestly marked as the *assumed unlock path* rather than papered over.
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* the table conflates "protected" levels of very different strength — "MITM with valid CA: Oui" rests on a *single hard-coded pin* whose private key, if lost, bricks the fleet or (worse) whose rotation story is incomplete; "Saisie PIN inconnu: Partiel" rests on Argon2id *plus* a Keystore MasterKey that is weak on rooted/no-TEE devices (R-D-1, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:664-667`). A single "Oui/Partiel" hides large variance in assurance. *Disposition — partially conceded.* The dossier does grade the residuals (R-D-1/R-D-2/R-C-2 device-dependent, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:656-679`) and marks Keystore-TEE as a trust assumption (`:704`). The remaining honesty gap is that the *headline* table reads more binary than the residual register; an auditor should read both together.
- **Assumptions & residual.** Each "Oui" carries the assumptions of its mechanism (TEE-backed Keystore, `--workers 1`, OsRng is a true CSPRNG, the shipped `.so` matches audited source — `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:696-712`). Does NOT claim protection where the row says **Hors** (rooted OS, Cellebrite firmware extraction).
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) For each "Oui" row, does the strength of the *weakest* underlying assumption match what a high-risk user would infer from the binary verdict (esp. the Keystore-dependent rows)? (2) Is the "MITM with valid CA: Oui" row still true under the *domain* migration that has already landed in code (see C7) — i.e. does the SNI/host-check path still reject a state-issued CA?

### C5 — Sovereign key = 12-word paper phrase; private read key on no machine

- **What it is.** At enrollment the device generates a BIP-39 12-word French phrase, shown once, never stored; it is the sole path to reading streams or recovering identity, and no authority (the project included) can reset it (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:120-139`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:29`). An optional 13th word derives a disjoint identity (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:134`). The X25519 read private key is *never* on the device — derivable only from the phrase (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:147-148`).
- **Affirmative case.** This is what makes recovery *sovereign and tthird-party-free*: phone destroyed/wiped → new device → 12 words → "RÉCUPÉRER MES STREAMS" → blobs descend and decrypt locally, "pas de compte, pas d'e-mail, pas de cloud, pas de tiers à supplier" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:126-129`). It is also what backs the blind-relay claim (C3): if the private read half lived anywhere online, the relay seizure claim would collapse. Concentrating failure on one offline physical artifact under human control is argued as *better* than diffusing it across "dix points logiciels" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:137-139`).
- **Why this implementation.** Alternatives — a server-held key (defeats blind relay), a cloud-escrow recovery (defeats sovereignty and adds a coercible third party), a device-bound key (defeats new-device recovery after wipe) — are each rejected by a named exigency in `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:48-59`. BIP-39 is chosen for transcribability to paper and standard, audited derivation (the frozen invariant: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512, 2048 iters, French NFD wordlist, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:131`).
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* this converts the whole system's security into a single physical secret — coercion on the phrase is "défaite complète," and a lost phrase means "archives définitivement illisibles" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:136-138`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:55`). For a coerced witness this is arguably *worse* than a vault, since there is no plausible-deniability or duress path. *Disposition — conceded by design, as a deliberate trade.* It is listed as "un point de défaillance total assumé" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:303-304`, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md` threat row "phrase = défaite"). The rebuttal is narrow: the design accepts phrase-coercion as out of scope *because* the alternative (diffuse software failure points) is judged strictly worse against the *primary* adversary (seizure + forensics), and the 13th-word disjoint identity offers a compartmentalization lever for those who prepare (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:134`).
- **Assumptions & residual.** Rests on the phrase being shown once and genuinely never persisted (a forensic on-device claim, `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:560-571`), on the witness physically protecting the paper, and on no duress/decoy mechanism being expected. Does NOT claim any protection if the phrase is coerced or lost.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) Confirm forensically that the mnemonic and the derived X25519 private key never touch disk or survive on the JVM heap after the one-time display (the heap-survival concern raised generally at `docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:551-557`). (2) Is there a place where the phrase is briefly reconstructable in RAM during recovery on the rescue device, and for how long?

### C6 — Deliberate out-of-scope: traffic envelope, OS-at-capture, chain-of-custody, burned domain, APK-in-state-hands

- **What it is.** Five explicit non-claims: (a) the *fact of emitting* (sizes/timing/IP) is not hidden — "Frappuccino n'est pas un anonymiseur réseau" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:295-298`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:52`); (b) a compromised OS at capture time "voit ce que voit le capteur" (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:299-300`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:53`); (c) no court-grade chain of custody (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:301`, `README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:54`); (d) rooted OS / Cellebrite firmware extraction are "Hors — game over, documenté" (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:116-117`); (e) a burned domain (DNS blocked/seized) needs an APK push and is outside the break-glass scope (`docs/TLS_PINNING_ROTATION_RUNBOOK.md:26`). Implicitly, an active state that holds and reverse-engineers the APK is not defended against beyond the published design (the pin is compiled into the `.so`, the protocol is open).
- **Affirmative case.** A tool for people in danger is judged by the honesty of its non-claims (`docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:291-293`). Scoping *out* the traffic envelope and OS-at-capture is defensible because they are genuinely unsolvable at this layer: no app defeats malware reading the screen, and hiding "you talk to this relay" requires a network layer (Tor/VPN) that is a different product — and Tor was *measured and rejected* as incompatible with sustained continuous video upload (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:129,159`). Declaring these out of scope steers users to combine with the right network layer rather than over-trusting the app.
- **Why this implementation.** The alternative — silently implying network anonymity or court-grade proof — would be the failure mode the whole transparency posture exists to avoid. Each non-claim points at the *complementary* tool that does cover it (ProofMode/eyeWitness for provenance, Tor/VPN for the network layer, `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:295-306,267-288`), which is more useful to a user than a false reassurance. The burned-domain case is scoped out and instead met with three pre-seeded pins for the *recoverable* sub-case (relay seizure, same domain — see C7), drawing a precise line between what break-glass can and cannot fix (`crypto-rs/stream/src/pin.rs:48-62`).
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection:* the traffic-envelope non-claim and the C2 metadata residual *interact* badly — a fixed bare destination plus an upload burst that "closely tracks the recording envelope" (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:66`) means the on-path adversary in scope (C4 row a) learns *when each session happened*, which is most of what a state needs. So an item declared "out of scope" (network anonymity) leaks a signal that partly defeats an item declared "in scope" (seizure exposes no testimony). *Disposition — conceded as a coherence tension (see §3).* The honest position is that the project ships an *obfuscation* layer (Salamander-wrapped QUIC) that buys *inclassifiability, not invisibility* — destination and timing/volume envelope unchanged (`README_FRAPPUCCINO.md:52`, `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md:354-358`) — and explicitly tells users to add a network layer if metadata itself endangers them.
- **Assumptions & residual.** Rests on users actually reading and acting on the non-claims; on the obfuscated transport being correctly understood as *not* anonymity; and on the burned-domain recovery requiring an out-of-band APK push that the project can deliver. Does NOT claim network anonymity, OS-integrity-at-capture, legal provenance, or survival of a fully burned domain without an APK update.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) Is the boundary between "obfuscation (shipped)" and "anonymity (out of scope)" stated clearly enough in *user-facing* surfaces that a high-risk user will not misread inclassifiability as invisibility? (2) For the active-state-holding-the-APK case: does anything in the design degrade gracefully when the adversary knows the pin, the protocol, and the relay IP — or is the entire posture predicated on the relay simply staying up and unmodified?

### C7 — The trust boundary: pinned-TLS to a single relay endpoint  ⚠️ *live doc-vs-code divergence*

- **What it is.** The motto's "tout quitte le téléphone" channel is a single TLS connection whose trust is reduced to an SPKI pin, *not* the system CA store (`crypto-rs/stream/src/pin.rs:36,209-248`; `mobile/src/main/res/xml/network_security_config.xml:28-61`). The verifier checks the host, then the SPKI SHA-256 against a *set* of pins (primary + two pre-seeded break-glass), and — post RT-01 — actually verifies the `CertificateVerify` signature (`pin.rs:250-271`, regression-tested at `:494-598`).
- **Affirmative case.** Pinning to the relay's own key, with the system CA store deliberately dropped as an anchor (`network_security_config.xml:18-27`), closes the "state-issued CA in the store MITMs the relay" window — a real threat for the target users. The three-pin union (`pin.rs:110-115`) is a thoughtful availability/security trade: it lets the relay key rotate, or recover from a *seizure* (the off-host third key never placed on the relay, `pin.rs:48-62`), *without* a flag-day APK brick — while each extra pin is "a pin WE control, not a CA fallback," so it does not widen MITM surface.
- **Why this implementation.** Alternatives: trust the system CA (rejected — a compelled CA defeats it); a single pin (rejected — any key loss bricks the fleet, the `HIGH` risk noted in `docs/TLS_PINNING_ROTATION_RUNBOOK.md:58`); rotating keys server-side under standard PKI (rejected pre-publication — needs a domain + LE, roadmap 1.2/1.3). The pin-set-of-three with off-host break-glass is the chosen middle path, validated by SPKI-match tests for all three certs (`pin.rs:372-425`).
- **Adversarial stress.** *Objection — the central finding of this domain:* the prose threat-model docs that ground the metadata posture (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:28,66,82` and `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md` lineage) repeatedly cite "**raw-IP pin, no SNI**" as a *deliberate anti-metadata choice* that avoids leaking a hostname in the TLS ClientHello — and warn that a future DNS/LE migration "introduces an SNI leak" as a *metadata regression* (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:117`). But the **live code has already migrated to a domain**: `PINNED_HOST = "relay.shake-document-protect.org"` (`crypto-rs/stream/src/pin.rs:69`) and `<domain>relay.shake-document-protect.org</domain>` (`network_security_config.xml:29`). The host-check in the verifier stringifies the SNI and compares it to that domain (`pin.rs:220-234`). So the SNI leak the docs frame as a *future, weighed* regression is, as of HEAD, *already shipped* — and `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md` (re-anchored 2026-06-27) still lists the no-SNI raw-IP design as a *current* control (`:28,82`). *Disposition — conceded, must be flagged.* This is a documentation-vs-code drift in exactly the domain (threat model & trust boundary) this review covers; the metadata map's own rule is "where the body and the code diverge, the code wins" (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:10`), so the live posture is: a cleartext SNI hostname is now on the wire, and the "connecting at all is a signal" non-claim (C6) is now *strictly worse* (the hostname, not just an IP, identifies the relay).
- **Assumptions & residual.** Rests on the operator still holding at least one pinned private key (the primary + the on-relay break-glass are both lost together in a seizure — hence the off-host third key, `pin.rs:53-58`); on the `.so` pin matching the NSC pin byte-for-byte (`docs/GUIDE_AUDITEUR.md:147-149`); and on the domain not being burned (out of scope, C6). Does NOT claim SNI/hostname privacy any longer, despite the prose docs.
- **Questions for the future audit.** (1) **Reconcile the SNI story:** is the migration to `relay.shake-document-protect.org` an accepted regression (and should `METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md §2 #1`, §4A and `POSITIONNEMENT` be corrected to stop claiming "no SNI"), or is a cleartext-hostname leak now in the field that the threat-model docs have not caught up to? (2) Does the host-check at `pin.rs:229` plus the three-pin union still reject an attacker who obtains *only* the public cert under the new domain (the RT-01 path) — i.e. is the C4 "MITM with valid CA: Oui" guarantee preserved across the domain flip? (3) Is the off-host break-glass private key (`PIN_NEXT2_B64`) custody documented and tested for the seizure-recovery cutover it exists for?

## 3. Domain coherence & tensions

The seven choices form a tight, mutually-reinforcing chain: **C5** (key on no machine) is what makes **C3** (blind relay) more than policy; **C3** is what makes **C1** (relay-not-vault) survive a server seizure; **C1+C3+C5** together are what let **C2** scope "seizure exposes nothing" to identity+content; **C4** indexes all of it to mechanisms and proofs; **C6** honestly fences off what the chain cannot reach; and **C7** is the single channel the whole chain depends on. The design is unusually coherent *because* it is built around one sentence (the motto) and then disciplined to scope every claim to what that sentence actually promises.

Three internal tensions are worth handing to the next audit:

1. **The metadata seam (C2 ↔ C6).** "Une saisie n'expose rien" (identity+content) and "the fact of emitting is not protected" are individually honest, but their *intersection* — a fixed destination + an upload envelope that tracks the recording envelope (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:66`) + a 6-month retention window (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:§6`) — reconstructs a per-`report_id` "when/how-long/how-much" timeline that, once correlated to a person on the wire, recovers much of what the identity-stripping removed at rest. The project knows this (it is the "highest-value militant risk", `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:62`) and chose throughput over padding; the tension is real, conceded, and the right thing for an external auditor to weigh against the target threat model.

2. **The doc-vs-code SNI divergence (C7).** The threat-model prose still sells a raw-IP/no-SNI design as a current anti-metadata control while the code ships a domain with a cleartext SNI. This is the one place where the *documentation* of the threat model is out of sync with the *implemented* trust boundary — directly in this review's domain. It does not necessarily mean a security regression was unweighed (the NSC comment at `network_security_config.xml:18-27,32-53` shows the domain/break-glass migration was deliberate and reasoned), but the metadata posture documents have not been re-reconciled, and a future auditor reading `METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md` would draw a *false* conclusion about what leaks on the wire.

3. **The single-artifact concentration (C5 ↔ C3).** Making the paper phrase the sole read path is the *source* of the blind-relay guarantee and *also* the system's single point of total failure. The two are inseparable: you cannot soften the phrase-loss/coercion residual without re-introducing an online key that weakens C3. This is a clean, defensible trade — but it means C5's residual cannot be "fixed" without trading away C3, and the audit should evaluate them as one coupled decision, not two.

## 4. Top-3 questions this domain hands to the future adversarial audit

1. **Reconcile the SNI / trust-boundary divergence.** The code pins a *domain* (`relay.shake-document-protect.org`, `crypto-rs/stream/src/pin.rs:69`, `network_security_config.xml:29`) and puts a cleartext hostname in every ClientHello, while `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:28,82` and `docs/POSITIONNEMENT.md` still present a "raw-IP, no-SNI" design as a *current* anti-metadata control and treat the SNI leak as a *future* regression. Is this an accepted, documented regression, or has a live on-wire identifier outrun the threat-model docs? Confirm too that the RT-01 MITM guarantee (C4 "valid-CA MITM: Oui") still holds under the domain flip.

2. **Does the directory/timeline residual quietly re-couple identity to activity (C2)?** Phase C stripped `owner_pk`/`createdAt` from `reports.json` (`server/app/routes/reports.py:18,23`), but the rescue **directory** aggregates all of a witness's session entries under one phrase-derived `report_id`, leaving an at-rest "N sessions at this cadence" object (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:114` non-claim M-1) plus a 6-month size/mtime fingerprint (`docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:§6`). How much of the "seizure exposes nothing" claim survives correlation of that residual with the on-wire timeline?

3. **Is the blind-relay "at-rest" boundary airtight against the transient and the exception channel (C3)?** The 1st-PUT carries a `sub=pk` JWT seen live by the relay (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:48,147`), and a pk/IP leak via exception/`repr`/positional log arg was *missed by two prior audit passes* (`docs/RELAY_BLIND_REPORTS.md:44,187`; `docs/METADATA_EXPOSURE_MAP.md:53,109`). Verify on the deployed wheel that no identity or IP reaches any persistent log under any error path, and that the transient pk is genuinely dropped, never co-logged with a `report_id`.
